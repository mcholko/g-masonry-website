Know Before You Call

Things You Need to Know About Tuckpointing

What is tuckpointing?

Tuckpointing is the repair of mortar joints in brick or stone walls. The old, failing mortar is ground out to a set depth and replaced with new mortar that's matched to the wall.

When should you tuckpoint?

If mortar looks recessed, cracked or sandy, it's time. A quick test: scrape a joint with a key. If it turns to powder, the mortar has failed.

Why does it matter?

Weak mortar lets water into the wall. That water freezes, expands and damages brick from the inside. Proper tuckpointing restores a watertight joint.

Signs Your Building Needs Masonry Repair

Mortar deterioration

Brick can last a century, but mortar wears out sooner. Crumbling joints are the clearest sign you need tuckpointing.

Frost damage

Wet ground freezes and expands against foundations. Catching it early keeps a small repair from becoming a structural one.

Bowed or bulging brick

A wall that bows outward usually needs more than tuckpointing. Call before it gets worse.

White, chalky stains

Efflorescence means water is moving through the wall. Find the source before it causes bigger damage.

Visible cracks

Step cracks and gaps in brick let water in and can weaken the wall over time.
